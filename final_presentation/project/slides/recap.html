We found no published work linking complaints to specific regulation sections.

~50 seconds. The problem: a compliance officer has 15 days to answer each CFPB consumer complaint, and must know which federal rule governs it. Input: the complaint text. Output: the governing regulation section, a draft reply that cites it, and every sentence marked supported or flagged. The tool assists an officer; it never replaces one. Prior work: Magesh et al. found that commercial legal AI tools (Lexis+ AI, Westlaw) still hallucinate 17 to 33 percent of the time, but they score the whole system, so you cannot tell whether retrieval or generation failed. RAGAS calls an answer faithful if it matches what was retrieved, which is not legal correctness. SummaC gives us sentence-level checking. What we add is the attribution experiment, in the diagram: draft every complaint twice, once from what our retriever found (score A) and once from the correct passage (score B). The difference B minus A is what retrieval costs; whatever is still wrong with the correct passage is generation's fault.
