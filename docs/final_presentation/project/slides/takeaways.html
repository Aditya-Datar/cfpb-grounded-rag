  • Rewriting makes drafts address the complaint less often (48% → 34%)

~40 seconds, then Q&A. Three takeaways. One: the attribution experiment tells practitioners where to spend effort, and the answer changed as the system improved: retrieval cost 70 points at the midterm and 11 now. Two: once retrieval is fixed, verification is the lever; checking and rewriting flagged sentences gave the largest single gain, 25 points. Three: 75 percent of drafts are acceptable. Magesh et al. report 65 percent for Lexis+ AI, but on open-ended legal research graded by lawyers, so this is the same range, not a head-to-head. Limitations: the rewrite step and the score share a checker, so part of the gain could be rewrites tuned to the checker; we are labelling 24 rewritten sentences by hand to test that. Rewriting also makes drafts address the complaint less often. Q&A prep: dev was used for every choice and held-out was run once per pre-registered rule; gold kappa 0.85 to 0.90; the checker cutoff was chosen on set 1 and tested on set 2.
